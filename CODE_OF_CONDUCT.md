# Code of conduct

RepoCanary exists for people who are being targeted. Many of them arrive
here frightened, early in their careers, and unsure whether they have just
lost everything. That sets the tone for this project.

## Expectations

- Be kind, and be precise. A report that says "RepoCanary got this wrong,
  here is the file" is welcome; a report that mocks the person who ran the
  repository is not.
- Nobody is stupid for having been fooled. These campaigns fool experienced
  engineers. Treat every "I think I ran it" post as an emergency for the
  person writing it, and point them at WHAT-TO-DO-NOW.md before anything
  else.
- Argue about rules with evidence: a fixture, a benchmark repository, a
  public write-up. Not with credentials or volume.
- Never post a working payload, a live command-and-control endpoint, or a
  real credential anywhere in this project, including in issues. Describe
  the technique or link to a public advisory instead.
- Respect that maintainers decline changes that would make the tool easier
  to evade, even when the change would make a result look tidier. The
  reasoning is in THREAT-MODEL.md; disagree with it there, on the merits.

## Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, publishing someone
else's private information, and any attempt to use this project to
distribute or improve malware. Any of these is grounds for removal from the
project's spaces, at the maintainers' discretion.

## Reporting

Email the maintainer at the address in package.json, or use GitHub's report
feature on the content in question. Reports are handled privately.

This document adapts the spirit of the Contributor Covenant to a project
whose users are, by definition, under attack.
